# Micro Desk

### Acquisition Memo 001 — Nichols plc (AIM: NICL)
**Date:** September 2026

---

## 1. Target Overview

Nichols plc is a UK-listed (AIM) diversified soft drinks group, best known for its Vimto brand. The business operates across three segments: **UK Packaged** (retail soft drinks), **International Packaged** (exports, notably strong in Africa and the Middle East, sold on a higher-margin concentrate model), and **Out of Home** (drinks dispensed in pubs, restaurants and venues via the Vimto and other brands).

**Key financials, FY2025 (year ended 31 December 2025):**
| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group Revenue | £175.1m | £172.8m | +1.3% |
| Adjusted Operating Profit | £31.7m | £28.9m | +9.9% |
| Adjusted Operating Margin | 18.1% | 16.7% | +140bps |
| Profit Before Tax | £29.2m | £24.0m | +21.5% |
| Earnings Per Share (basic) | 58.67p | 48.84p | +20.1% |
| Cash and Cash Equivalents | £55.7m | £53.7m | +3.8% |

Nichols is essentially **debt-free with a substantial net cash position**, and has grown revenue every year since 2021 while expanding margin. Market cap as of late September 2026 sits at roughly **£355–370m**, implying an **enterprise value of approximately £300–315m** once net cash is stripped out.

---

## 2. Strategic Rationale

Nichols fits the classic profile of a search-fund-style target, even though at its current size it would realistically be pursued by a strategic acquirer or larger PE fund rather than an individual search-fund operator:

- **Stable, cash-generative, unglamorous business.** Soft drinks concentrate and distribution is not a hyped sector, which is exactly why it tends to be overlooked and reasonably priced relative to its cash generation.
- **Founder/family influence.** The Nichols family has long held a significant shareholding in the business, which has historically meant conservative balance sheet management (net cash, not net debt) and a long-term operating horizon rather than short-term earnings management.
- **International growth optionality.** The International Packaged segment, particularly Africa, is growing on a higher-margin, capital-light concentrate licensing model — a segment a new owner could accelerate investment into without needing to rebuild the core UK business from scratch.
- **Genuine free cash flow.** H1 2025 free cash flow was reported at £14.2m, up from £9.0m in H1 2024 — a business that is not just profitable on paper but converting that profit into distributable cash.

## 3. Valuation

Using FY2025 figures and a share price of approximately 970p (giving a market cap of roughly £355–367m):

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Market Capitalisation | ~£360m |
| Less: Net Cash | ~£55.7m |
| **Implied Enterprise Value** | **~£300–305m** |
| Adjusted Operating Profit (FY25) | £31.7m |
| **EV / Adjusted Operating Profit** | **~9.5x** |
| Adjusted EPS (FY25) | 67.53p |
| **P/E (on adjusted EPS)** | **~14.4x** |

An EV/Operating Profit multiple in the high single digits, for a business growing revenue and expanding margin with no debt, is not an obviously demanding valuation for a strategic or financial acquirer — particularly against branded consumer peers, which often trade at a premium to this. This is a starting observation, not a final judgement — a full analysis would benchmark this more rigorously against a proper set of listed soft drinks/branded FMCG comparables.

---

## 4. Deal Structure (Illustrative)

Given Nichols' net cash position and modest leverage capacity already available on the balance sheet, an illustrative structure might look like:

- **Purchase price:** ~£360m (at or near current market value, reflecting the quality and stability of the asset — this is not framed as a distressed or opportunistic acquisition)
- **Funding:** existing net cash (£55.7m) applied directly to reduce the acquisition financing need, remainder funded through a mix of acquisition debt (modest leverage, given the business's low existing leverage and strong FCF conversion) and equity
- **Note:** at this size (£300m+ enterprise value), this sits above what an individual search fund would typically pursue on its own — more realistically a target for a mid-market PE fund or a strategic trade acquirer (a larger branded beverages group). This memo treats it as an illustrative valuation and structuring exercise regardless.

---

## 5. Value Creation Plan

- **Accelerate the International Packaged segment** specifically, given its higher-margin, asset-light concentrate model and evidence of real growth in Africa.
- **Out of Home segment expansion** — post-pandemic recovery in hospitality volumes suggests further room to grow this channel, which carries different (often favourable) margin dynamics versus retail packaged.
- **Capital allocation discipline** — with a large net cash balance already sitting on the balance sheet, a new owner (particularly a PE sponsor) could apply that cash toward a bolt-on acquisition strategy in adjacent beverage categories, rather than it sitting idle.

---

## 6. Key Risks

- **Brand concentration risk** — Vimto is the dominant brand; the business is less diversified than its "diversified soft drinks group" description might suggest at first glance.
- **UK consumer/retail pricing pressure** — supermarket own-label competition and retailer margin pressure are structural risks to the UK Packaged segment specifically.
- **Family shareholding** — a significant family stake could mean the business is not readily available for acquisition at all, regardless of price, if the family is not a willing seller. This is arguably the single biggest practical risk to the entire thesis, separate from any valuation question.
- **FX exposure** — International Packaged revenue, particularly Africa and Middle East, carries currency risk that could distort reported growth.

---

## 7. Verdict

**Pursue further diligence, not immediate pursuit at current price.** The business exhibits genuinely attractive fundamentals — stable growth, expanding margins, strong free cash flow conversion, and a debt-free balance sheet — but the significant family shareholding is a real practical constraint on deliverability that would need addressing before this progresses from a valuation exercise to an actual approach. The next step would be a more rigorous comparable company analysis against listed branded beverage peers to sharpen the valuation view, and a closer look at actual shareholder register data to assess realistic deal feasibility.
